\subsection{Shape-factor sensitivity analysis}
\label{si:shapefactor}

Figure~\ref{fig:S1} examines the sensitivity of the three-phase Lewis--Nielsen
predictions to the h-BN shape factor $A$ over the range 0.3--6.0
(needle-like rods to aligned platelets), evaluated at the NP mean porosity
of 65.6\% with $\phimax = 0.60$ fixed. For the dielectric model
(Figure~\ref{fig:S1}a), all $A$ curves are nearly indistinguishable,

\SI{0.305}{\watt\per\meter\per\kelvin}), reinforcing that the 70~wt\%
enhancement is driven by proximity to maximum packing and incipient
particle contact rather than by any plausible single-particle shape factor.
Full discussion appears in Sections~3.2 and 4.6 of the main text.

\section{Model details and benchmarks}
\label{si:model}

\subsection{Sequential three-phase Lewis--Nielsen formulation}
\label{si:formulation}
The Lewis--Nielsen model gives the effective scalar transport property $k$ of a two-phase particulate composite with matrix property $k_m$, filler property $k_f$, and filler volume fraction $\phi$ as
\begin{equation}
\frac{k}{k_m} = \frac{1 + A\,B\,\phi}{1 - B\,\psi\,\phi},
\label{eq:S1}
\end{equation}
\begin{equation}
B = \frac{k_f/k_m - 1}{k_f/k_m + A},
\label{eq:S2}
\end{equation}
\begin{equation}
\psi = 1 + \frac{1 - \phimax}{\phimax^{2}}\,\phi ,
\label{eq:S3}
\end{equation}
where $A$ is the Einstein coefficient (shape factor) of the dispersed phase and \phimax{} its maximum packing fraction. Eq.~\ref{eq:S1} has three useful limits. For $A \to 0$ it reduces to the series (inverse rule-of-mixtures) model, $k^{-1} = (1-\phi)/k_m + \phi/k_f$ when $\psi \to 1$; for $A \to \infty$ it reduces to the parallel rule of mixtures; and for $A = 1.5$, $\psi = 1$ it is the Maxwell--Garnett expression for dilute spheres. The shape factor therefore interpolates between the Wiener bounds, and a small fitted $A$ means that the dispersed phase acts nearly in series with the matrix.

For the porous composite the model is applied twice. Step~1 mixes the PI matrix with h-BN at the solid-phase volume fraction \phiBN{} (Section~\ref{si:volfrac}):
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
k_{\mathrm{dense}} &= k_{\mathrm{PI}}\,\frac{1 + \ABN\,B\,\phiBN}{1 - B\,\psi\,\phiBN}, \\
B &= \frac{k_{\mathrm{BN}}/k_{\mathrm{PI}} - 1}{k_{\mathrm{BN}}/k_{\mathrm{PI}} + \ABN}, \qquad
\psi = 1 + \frac{1-\phi_{\max,\mathrm{BN}}}{\phi_{\max,\mathrm{BN}}^{2}}\,\phiBN .
\end{aligned}
\label{eq:S4}
\end{equation}
Step~2 introduces the air pores at porosity $\phi_p$ into the dense composite:
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
k_{\mathrm{film}} &= k_{\mathrm{dense}}\,\frac{1 + \Apore\,B_p\,\phi_p}{1 - B_p\,\psi_p\,\phi_p}, \\
B_p &= \frac{k_{\mathrm{air}}/k_{\mathrm{dense}} - 1}{k_{\mathrm{air}}/k_{\mathrm{dense}} + \Apore}, \qquad
\psi_p = 1 + \frac{1-\phi_{\max,\mathrm{pore}}}{\phi_{\max,\mathrm{pore}}^{2}}\,\phi_p .
\end{aligned}
\label{eq:S5}
\end{equation}
Because $k_{\mathrm{air}} < k_{\mathrm{dense}}$, $B_p$ is negative and step~2 always lowers the property. The same equations are used for the dielectric constant ($k = \varepsilon$: $\varepsilon_{\mathrm{PI}} = 3.5$, $\varepsilon_{\mathrm{BN}} = 6.0$, $\varepsilon_{\mathrm{air}} = 1.0$) and for the thermal conductivity ($k = \lambda$: $\lambda_{\mathrm{PI}} = 0.12$, $\lambda_{\mathrm{air}} = \SI{0.026}{\watt\per\meter\per\kelvin}$, $\lambda_{\mathrm{BN}} = \lamBN$ fitted).

The film-level volume fractions used by the zero-parameter mixing rules of Section~\ref{si:mixingrules} are
\begin{equation}
f_{\mathrm{air}} = \phi_p, \qquad f_{\mathrm{BN}} = \phiBN\,(1 - \phi_p), \qquad f_{\mathrm{PI}} = (1 - \phiBN)(1 - \phi_p).
\label{eq:S6}
\end{equation}
The residual of film $i$ in state $s$ is
\begin{equation}
r_{i,s} = \varepsilon_{r,\mathrm{model}}(\phiBN[i], \phi_{p}[i,s];\,\mathbf{p}) - \varepsilon_{r,\mathrm{meas}}[i,s],
\label{eq:S7}
\end{equation}
and the dielectric parameters $\mathbf{p} = (\ABN, \phi_{\max,\mathrm{BN}}, \Apore, \phi_{\max,\mathrm{pore}})$ are obtained by minimizing the mean squared residual over the ten films,
\begin{equation}
\mathcal{L}(\mathbf{p}) = \frac{1}{10}\sum_{s \in \{\mathrm{NP},\mathrm{HP}\}}\sum_{i=1}^{5} r_{i,s}^{2}, \qquad \mathrm{RMSE} = \sqrt{\mathcal{L}} ,
\label{eq:S8}
\end{equation}
subject to the bounds $0.05 \le A \le 20$ and $0.30 \le \phimax \le 0.99$. The thermal objective has the same form over the seven films with measured \lam.

\subsection{Volume-fraction conversion}
\label{si:volfrac}
The h-BN weight fraction of the solids, $w$, was converted to the solid-phase volume fraction with $\rho_{\mathrm{PI}} = \SI{1.42}{\gram\per\cubic\centi\meter}$ and $\rho_{\mathrm{BN}} = \SI{2.29}{\gram\per\cubic\centi\meter}$,
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
\phiBN &= \frac{w/\rho_{\mathrm{BN}}}{w/\rho_{\mathrm{BN}} + (1-w)/\rho_{\mathrm{PI}}}, \\
\rho_{\mathrm{solid}} &= \left(\frac{w}{\rho_{\mathrm{BN}}} + \frac{1-w}{\rho_{\mathrm{PI}}}\right)^{-1}, \qquad
\phi_{\mathrm{total}} = 1 - \frac{\rho}{\rho_{\mathrm{solid}}},
\end{aligned}
\label{eq:S9}
\end{equation}
where $\rho$ is the measured bulk density (Table~3 of the main text). Table~\ref{tab:S2} lists the resulting fractions for all ten films.

\subsection{Sensitivity of the dielectric fit to the porosity definition}
\label{si:porosityfit}
The main-text calibration uses the open (intrusion) porosity, which is available for all ten films. Because \eps{} responds to the total air fraction, we re-fitted the dielectric model with $\phi_{\mathrm{total}}$ as the porosity input for the seven films with measured density (Table~\ref{tab:S3}). With the open porosity, the main-text parameter set gives RMSE 0.31 and $R^2 = -0.9$ on these seven films; with the total porosity, the re-fit gives RMSE 0.24 and $R^2 = -0.2$. The porosity definition therefore accounts for a substantial part of the residual, as stated in Section~4.2 of the main text, although the model still does not outperform the data mean. The re-fit moves the pore shape factor from 0.08 to 0.43 (with \ABN{} again weakly determined), which remains well below the isolated-sphere value of 1.5, so the qualitative conclusion that the NIPS sponge shields the field far less efficiently than spherical pores holds for either porosity definition; the numerical value of \Apore{} quoted in the main text is specific to the open-porosity input. The sensitivity of the model to the porosity input is $\partial\eps/\partial\phi_p \approx -1.7$ to $-2.2$ per unit porosity at 50--70~wt\%, so an uncertainty of 3 percentage points in $\phi_p$ corresponds to 0.05--0.07 in \eps, and the 21--31 percentage-point gap between open and total porosity at 30--70~wt\% NP corresponds to 0.4--0.6.

\subsection{Uncertainty propagation}
\label{si:uncertainty}
\emph{Thermal conductivity.} With $\lam = \alpha\rho C_p$ and independent relative uncertainties of 5\% ($\alpha$, laser flash), 3\% ($C_p$, DSC) and 8\% ($\rho$, dominated by the thickness measurement of a porous film), the propagated relative uncertainty is $\sqrt{0.05^2 + 0.03^2 + 0.08^2} = 9.9\%$, i.e.\ about $\pm$\SI{0.03}{\watt\per\meter\per\kelvin} at 70~wt\% and $\pm$0.005--\SI{0.01}{\watt\per\meter\per\kelvin} at 10--50~wt\%. The $+16\%$ residual of the NP 70~wt\% film and the $-8.5\%$ change on hot-pressing (Section~4.3) are therefore comparable to, not larger than, the measurement uncertainty, and are interpreted as indicative only.

\emph{Dielectric constant.} The repeatability of the in-house LCR measurement is about $\pm$\hl{0.05} ($\pm 3\%$). The inter-laboratory comparison on the HP 30~wt\% film (1.864 in-house vs.\ 1.610 at KOPTRI, $-13.6\%$) is a better estimate of the reproducibility for porous films with contact electrodes, because it includes the electrode--surface air gap discussed in Section~4.2. We therefore regard model residuals below $\sim$14\% as within the experimental reproducibility; residuals of 19--38\% (Table~2 of the main text) exceed it.

\emph{Porosity.} The repeatability of mercury intrusion is about $\pm 2$ percentage points. The density-derived total porosity inherits the 8\% uncertainty of $\rho$: $\delta\phi_{\mathrm{total}} = 0.08\,\rho/\rho_{\mathrm{solid}} = 2$--5 percentage points (largest for the densest film, HP 10~wt\%). Through the sensitivity quoted in Section~\ref{si:porosityfit}, both correspond to $\pm$0.04--0.1 in the model \eps.

\emph{CTE.} The TMA repeatability is about $\pm$\hl{1}~\si{\ppm\per\kelvin}, small compared with the 15--30~\si{\ppm\per\kelvin} separation between the measured values and the Turner and rule-of-mixtures bounds (Table~4 of the main text).

\subsection{Benchmark against standard mixing rules}
\label{si:mixingrules}
Table~\ref{tab:S4} compares the calibrated three-phase LN model with the zero-parameter three-component mixing rules evaluated with the film volume fractions of Eq.~\ref{eq:S6}: the Lichtenecker logarithmic rule, $\ln\varepsilon = \sum_j f_j \ln\varepsilon_j$; the Maxwell--Garnett rule with PI as the host and h-BN and air as inclusions; the symmetric Bruggeman rule, $\sum_j f_j(\varepsilon_j - \varepsilon)/(\varepsilon_j + 2\varepsilon) = 0$; and, as bounds, the parallel (Wiener upper) and series (Wiener lower) rules. Over the 30--70~wt\% NP films, the range in which the model is used for design, the calibrated LN model gives RMSE 0.26 against 0.55 (Lichtenecker), 0.73 (Bruggeman) and 0.85 (Maxwell--Garnett); over all ten films the corresponding values are 0.39 against 0.48, 0.58 and 0.64. All three symmetric or host-based rules overpredict \eps{} because they treat the pores as inclusions that only partly interrupt the field. The series bound, which corresponds to the $A \to 0$ limit of Eq.~\ref{eq:S1}, lies closest to the NP data at 30--70~wt\% (RMSE 0.17); the fitted $\Apore = 0.08$ places the pore step of the LN model close to this limit, which is the quantitative expression of the statement in Section~3.2 of the main text that the bicontinuous sponge pores act nearly in series with the PI skeleton.

\section{Parameter estimation protocol}
\label{si:paramest}

\subsection{Dielectric calibration}
\label{si:dielfit}
The objective of Eq.~\ref{eq:S8} was minimized with the Nelder--Mead simplex algorithm (SciPy \texttt{optimize.minimize}; $x_{\mathrm{tol}} = 10^{-6}$, $f_{\mathrm{tol}} = 10^{-9}$, at most 4000 iterations) from 16 random starting points drawn uniformly from $\ABN \in [0.3, 6]$, $\Apore \in [0.02, 2]$ and $\phimax \in [0.5, 0.99]$ for both phases; parameter sets outside the bounds of Section~\ref{si:formulation} were penalized. All starts converged to the same ridge: both packing fractions run to the upper bound of 0.99, \Apore{} lies at 0.07--0.09, and the objective is nearly flat in \ABN{} (Table~\ref{tab:S5}). Over $\ABN = 0.05$--6 the RMSE varies by only 0.003, so the dielectric data determine \Apore{} but not \ABN; the value $\ABN = 0.68$ quoted in the main text lies on this ridge and is reported with that qualification. Fixing $\phi_{\max,\mathrm{BN}}$ anywhere between 0.50 and 0.99 and re-fitting the remaining parameters changes the RMSE by less than 0.01 (Table~\ref{tab:S6}), which is why the packing fractions are described as inactive. With the main-text parameter set the model reproduces the NP and HP data with RMSE 0.43 and 0.36, against standard deviations of the measured values of 0.19 and 0.18, so $R^2 = -3.9$ (NP) and $-3.0$ (HP).

\subsection{Thermal calibration and the degeneracy of the unconstrained fit}
\label{si:degenerate}
The thermal model has three step-1 parameters ($\lamBN$, $A_{\mathrm{BN},\lambda}$, $\phi_{\max,\mathrm{BN},\lambda}$); the pore step uses the dielectric pore parameters ($\Apore = 0.08$, $\phi_{\max,\mathrm{pore}} = 0.99$), since the pore geometry is a property of the film, not of the transported quantity. With $A_{\mathrm{BN},\lambda} = 5.5$ and $\phi_{\max,\mathrm{BN},\lambda} = 0.60$ fixed, the single-parameter fit to the seven measured conductivities gives $\lamBN = \SI{6.65}{\watt\per\meter\per\kelvin}$ with $\lambda_{\mathrm{air}} = \SI{0.026}{\watt\per\meter\per\kelvin}$ (6.81 with $\lambda_{\mathrm{air}} = 0.023$; the predicted film conductivities differ by less than \SI{0.001}{\watt\per\meter\per\kelvin} between the two), RMSE 0.027 (NP) and \SI{0.030}{\watt\per\meter\per\kelvin} (HP), and predictions of 0.262 and \SI{0.315}{\watt\per\meter\per\kelvin} for the NP and HP 70~wt\% films.

When all three parameters are released, the multi-start optimizer runs to the edge of the admissible region: $A_{\mathrm{BN},\lambda}$ increases without bound (it reaches the upper limit of 2000 imposed in the code), $\phi_{\max,\mathrm{BN},\lambda}$ falls to 0.06, $\lamBN$ falls to \SI{1.9}{\watt\per\meter\per\kelvin}, and the RMSE improves only from 0.027/0.030 to 0.025 (pooled). Table~\ref{tab:S7} shows why: for any fixed $A_{\mathrm{BN},\lambda}$ between 20 and 700 there is a compensating $\phi_{\max,\mathrm{BN},\lambda}$ that gives the same pooled RMSE to within 0.0002, because in Eq.~\ref{eq:S4} a large $A$ and a small \phimax{} both amplify the filler contribution near the 70~wt\% point and the seven data cannot separate them. The unconstrained solution ($A \sim 10^3$, $\phimax \sim 0.1$) has no physical meaning for a platelet filler, whereas the constrained solution uses literature values for randomly oriented platelets and reproduces the data equally well. This is the basis for the single-parameter calibration adopted in the main text.
